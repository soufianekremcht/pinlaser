# Content Matrix

A skill for generating a structured Content Matrix: a table that crosses content **types** (X-axis) with content **topics** (Y-axis), with a specific, tailored content idea in every cell.

## Purpose

Help the user generate smart, original, and strategically varied content ideas using a structured Content Matrix framework that crosses content types with topics.

## Behavior Instructions

Follow these steps in order:

1. **Learn about the user.** Ask the user to describe who they are, what they do, and what they like to talk about. Ask for at least 2+ detailed paragraphs — the more specific, the better the matrix will be.
2. **Get topics.** Ask the user for 3–4 content topics they want help ideating on (e.g., Entrepreneurship, AI, Branding).
3. **Generate the Content Matrix.** Build a table with:
   - **X-axis:** Content types (see list below)
   - **Y-axis:** The user's topics
4. **Fill every cell.** At each intersection of a content type and a topic, write one specific content idea tailored to the user's voice, audience, and expertise. Avoid generic placeholders — the idea should read as something only this user could have made.
5. **Stay strategic.** Avoid surface-level or generic advice. Assume the user is a creator or founder who values originality, clarity, and growth. Every idea should have a clear angle, not just a topic restated as a title.

## Content Matrix Structure

### X-Axis: Content Types

1. **[Actionable]** – Specific "how to" guides or tutorials.
2. **[Motivational]** – Stories that inspire or show what's possible.
3. **[Analytical]** – Breakdowns and deep explanations of why things work.
4. **[Contrarian]** – Challenges common beliefs and flips the script.
5. **[Observation]** – Hidden trends or overlooked patterns.
6. **[X vs. Y]** – Comparisons (tools, ideas, paths, etc.).
7. **[Present vs. Future]** – Contrasts now vs. what's next.
8. **[Listicle]** – Curated, digestible lists of resources or insights.

### Y-Axis: Content Topics

Provided by the user (e.g., Entrepreneurship, AI, Branding, etc.).

## Output Format

Present the Content Matrix as a table:

| Topic | Actionable | Motivational | Analytical | Contrarian | Observation | X vs. Y | Present vs. Future | Listicle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Topic 1 | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea |
| Topic 2 | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea | idea |

Each cell's idea should be a short, specific, ready-to-use content concept (a working title or hook), not a vague description of the content type.

## Quality Bar

- Every idea must be tailored to the specific user's voice, audience, and expertise — not generic advice that could apply to anyone in their industry.
- Avoid restating the topic; each idea needs a distinct angle appropriate to its content type.
- If the user's self-description is thin, ask a follow-up before generating the matrix rather than guessing.
